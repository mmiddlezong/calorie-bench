How well can AI count calories from a photo?

Models see one overhead photo of a real cafeteria plate and estimate its calories, weight and macros. Every answer is checked against ground truth from the Nutrition5k dataset, where each ingredient was weighed as the plate was built.

Method

Full details, code and raw predictions are on GitHub.

Dataset

100 plates from the official Nutrition5k test split, stratified by calorie decile (31–902 kcal). Labels are computed from weighed ingredients. Plates with inconsistent labels or food out of frame were excluded.

Task

One overhead PNG photo and one fixed prompt. The model lists each food with estimated grams and calories, then gives plate totals as JSON. Every model runs at high reasoning effort.

Scoring

Headline: mean absolute calorie error, with bootstrap 95% CIs. A failed or refused answer counts as predicting 0 kcal. API errors are retried, never scored.

Caveats

100 dishes is small: check confidence intervals before claiming a winner. One cuisine, one camera angle, no scale reference. Ground truth uses database nutrition values, not lab assays.
