How well can AI count calories from a photo?

We showed AI models 200 photos of real cafeteria plates and asked how many calories were on each one. The right answers come from Nutrition5k, where every ingredient was weighed as the plate was put together.

Method

The plates. 200 dishes from the official Nutrition5k test set, the same number from each tenth of the calorie range (31 to 942 kcal). Plates whose labels didn't add up, or where food was out of frame, were left out.

The question. Each model gets one overhead photo and the same prompt: list the foods with their weights and calories, then give totals for the plate. Every model runs at its high reasoning setting.

Scoring. The main number is the average gap between a model's calorie estimate and the weighed truth. The ranges are 95% confidence intervals, so models whose ranges overlap may not really differ. An answer that fails or is refused counts as a guess of zero.

Limits. This is still a small sample, all from one cafeteria and one camera angle, with nothing in the photo for scale. The truth comes from weighed ingredients and nutrition databases, not lab tests. Code, prompt and every raw answer are on GitHub.
